# Refalo et al. 2021 load meta-analysis

- Source ID: refalo_2021_load
- Title: Influence of resistance training load on measures of skeletal muscle hypertrophy and improvements in maximal strength and neuromuscular task performance: A systematic review and meta-analysis
- Authors/organization: Martin C Refalo et al.
- Publication year: 2021
- Original URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33874848/
- DOI: 10.1080/02640414.2021.1898094; PMID: 33874848
- Source type: systematic review and meta-analysis
- Topic: higher versus lower external loads
- Studied population: participants from 45 studies comparing higher and lower training loads.
- Verified: 2026-10-23

## Findings reported by the source

Higher- and lower-load training produced similar average hypertrophy across whole-body, whole-muscle, and muscle-fiber measures. Higher loads produced greater one-repetition-maximum and isometric strength gains. The review defined higher load as more than 60% of one-repetition maximum or fewer than 15-repetition maximum, so its cutoffs differ from other syntheses.

## Practical interpretation

Muscle growth need not be confined to one rep or load range. Include heavier practice when maximal strength is a priority; select other loads based on exercise suitability, effort, and tolerability.

## Limitations and uncertainty

“Similar average hypertrophy” is not proof of identical results for every person or exercise. Outcome measures and training protocols differed, and the effect of load on other athletic tasks remained unclear.
