## Framework described by NASM

NASM describes four phases: inhibit, lengthen, activate, and integrate. It frames these as a sequence for addressing observed movement compensations in a training setting. The article explicitly distinguishes fitness work from medical conditions and directs pain, numbness, or suspected pathology to qualified health professionals.

## Practical interpretation

A trainer may use the sequence as an educational way to plan movement practice within scope. It should not be presented as a diagnosis, a rehabilitation protocol, or a guarantee of injury prevention.

## Limitations and uncertainty

The page does not supply controlled evidence that this exact sequence is necessary, superior to alternatives, or predicts injury. The movement-screening review in this collection reports insufficient evidence to label available screens as injury-prediction tools. A visible movement difference alone does not establish tissue pathology.
