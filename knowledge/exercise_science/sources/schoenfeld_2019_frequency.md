# Schoenfeld et al. 2019 training frequency meta-analysis

- Source ID: schoenfeld_2019_frequency
- Title: How many times per week should a muscle be trained to maximize muscle hypertrophy? A systematic review and meta-analysis of studies examining the effects of resistance training frequency
- Authors/organization: Brad Jon Schoenfeld, Jozo Grgic, James Krieger
- Publication year: 2019
- Original URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30558493/
- DOI: 10.1080/02640414.2018.1555906; PMID: 30558493
- Source type: systematic review and meta-analysis
- Topic: frequency per muscle group and hypertrophy
- Studied population: participants in 25 resistance-training studies included in the synthesis.
- Verified: 2026-10-23

## Findings reported by the source

When weekly volume was equated, higher versus lower muscle-training frequency did not significantly or meaningfully change hypertrophy. In studies without volume matching, a modest association favored higher frequency. This distinction matters: more days can also mean more total work.

## Practical interpretation

Distributing a given weekly volume across a schedule that a person can perform consistently is reasonable. Frequency can help organize work without being assumed to have a large independent growth effect.

## Limitations and uncertainty

The pooled studies differed in training status, measurement, and design. A null average effect does not prove all schedules equivalent for every individual or exercise. Frequency may affect strength or manageability differently from hypertrophy.
