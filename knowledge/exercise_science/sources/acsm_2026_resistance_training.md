## Findings reported by the source

The overview synthesized 137 systematic reviews involving more than 30,000 participants. Resistance training improved several muscle and physical-function outcomes compared with no exercise. Among the reviewed prescription variables, heavier loads, complete range of motion, and at least two weekly sessions favored voluntary strength. Higher weekly set volumes, including the category of at least ten sets, favored hypertrophy. Training to momentary fatigue and several other commonly debated variables did not consistently change outcomes across reviews.

## Practical interpretation

Progressive resistance training is the broad recommendation. A strength goal can justify including heavier loads; a hypertrophy goal can justify considering more weekly work if it is tolerable. The reported set threshold is an evidence-summary category, not an exact universal optimum or a required starting dose.

## Limitations and uncertainty

This is an overview of reviews, not a new trial. The included interventions and populations varied, and the abstract cannot establish an individualized prescription. It does not support diagnosing injury or prescribing rehabilitation.
