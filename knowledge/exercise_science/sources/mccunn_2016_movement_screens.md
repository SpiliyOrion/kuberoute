# McCunn et al. 2016 movement-screening critical review

- Source ID: mccunn_2016_screens
- Title: Reliability and Association with Injury of Movement Screens: A Critical Review
- Authors/organization: Robert McCunn et al.
- Publication year: 2016
- Original URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26721517/
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-015-0453-1; PMID: 26721517
- Source type: critical review
- Topic: movement-screen reliability and association with injury
- Studied population: athletes and other participants in the reviewed movement-screen studies; this is not a clinical diagnostic trial.
- Verified: 2026-10-23

## Findings reported by the source

The reviewers found reliability data for ten screens, but only two had evidence directly related to injury risk. Small samples, unclear injury definitions, missing exposure information, and bias limited inference. They concluded that available screens lacked enough evidence to be called injury-prediction tools.

## Practical interpretation

A movement observation may help a trainer discuss exercise technique, but it should not be used alone to predict injury or diagnose a condition. Pain or suspected pathology calls for appropriate medical assessment.

## Limitations and uncertainty

This review does not show that all movement coaching is ineffective. It addresses the evidence for screen reliability and injury association available at the time, and does not validate NASM's corrective continuum as an injury-prevention intervention.
