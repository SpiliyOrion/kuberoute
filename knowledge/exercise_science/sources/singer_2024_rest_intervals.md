# Singer et al. 2024 rest-interval meta-analysis

- Source ID: singer_2024_rest
- Title: Give it a rest: a systematic review with Bayesian meta-analysis on the effect of inter-set rest interval duration on muscle hypertrophy
- Authors/organization: Alec Singer et al.
- Publication year: 2024
- Original URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39205815/
- DOI: 10.3389/fspor.2024.1429789; PMID: 39205815
- Source type: systematic review and Bayesian meta-analysis
- Topic: between-set rest duration and hypertrophy
- Studied population: healthy adults in nine randomized studies meeting the review's criteria.
- Verified: 2026-10-23

## Findings reported by the source

The synthesis suggested a small possible hypertrophy advantage for resting more than 60 seconds between sets, while estimates across rest categories overlapped substantially. It did not detect appreciable additional hypertrophy from resting beyond 90 seconds. Results varied by measurement site, and the analysis reported heterogeneity.

## Practical interpretation

Avoid assuming that very short rests are inherently better for growth. Allowing enough recovery to sustain useful work is reasonable; more rest is not proven to yield ever-increasing gains.

## Limitations and uncertainty

Only nine studies contributed. Different rest categories and body-site measurements limit precision. These results do not define one ideal rest duration for every exercise, goal, or person.
