# Pelland et al. 2026 volume and frequency meta-regressions

- Source ID: pelland_2026_volume_frequency
- Title: The Resistance Training Dose Response: Meta-Regressions Exploring the Effects of Weekly Volume and Frequency on Muscle Hypertrophy and Strength Gains
- Authors/organization: Joshua C Pelland et al.
- Publication year: 2026 (online publication December 2025)
- Original URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41343037/
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-025-02344-w; PMID: 41343037
- Source type: meta-analysis and meta-regression
- Topic: weekly sets, frequency, hypertrophy, and strength
- Studied population: 67 studies involving 2,058 participants; the reported sample was predominantly young men.
- Verified: 2026-10-23

## Findings reported by the source

The models associated more weekly sets with more hypertrophy and strength, with diminishing returns; the strength curve flattened more strongly. Frequency showed a consistently identifiable relationship with strength, again with diminishing returns. The frequency relationship for hypertrophy was compatible with negligible effects. The authors distinguished direct and indirect sets, using fractional weighting in their primary models.

## Practical interpretation

Count weekly work and its relevance to the target muscle before treating session frequency as a separate muscle-growth dose. More work may help, but marginal benefit can shrink while time and recovery costs rise.

## Limitations and uncertainty

Meta-regression is observational across studies and cannot set a precise optimum for one person. Set counting, program duration, training status, and the male-heavy sample affect transferability. The findings should not be summarized as “exactly twelve sets are best.”
