# Schoenfeld et al. 2017 weekly volume meta-analysis

- Source ID: schoenfeld_2017_volume
- Title: Dose-response relationship between weekly resistance training volume and increases in muscle mass: A systematic review and meta-analysis
- Authors/organization: Brad J Schoenfeld, Dan Ogborn, James W Krieger
- Publication year: 2017 (online publication 2016)
- Original URL: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27433992/
- DOI: 10.1080/02640414.2016.1210197; PMID: 27433992
- Source type: systematic review and meta-analysis
- Topic: weekly sets and muscle growth
- Studied population: 34 treatment groups from 15 resistance-training studies.
- Verified: 2026-10-23

## Findings reported by the source

The analysis associated more weekly sets with greater muscle-size changes. Its continuous model estimated a small average increase per additional set. Its categories of fewer than five, five to nine, and at least ten sets per muscle showed a trend, rather than a precise individual optimum.

## Practical interpretation

Weekly sets are a useful programming variable, and adding sets may increase growth for some people. Increase work only as tolerable and useful; the study does not justify prescribing a single set count for everyone.

## Limitations and uncertainty

The evidence base was modest and heterogeneous. Between-study associations do not identify a safe maximum, a minimum effective dose, or the ideal number of sets for a particular person. Later meta-regression in this collection suggests diminishing returns.
